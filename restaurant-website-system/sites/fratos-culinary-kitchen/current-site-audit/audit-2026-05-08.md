## What to preserve

1. **Big, ownable story:** "Turning Culinary Students into Chefs" is more memorable than a standard pizza-shop pitch and should lead the redesign narrative.
2. **Food specificity:** massive mozzarella sticks, tavern/stone-oven pizza, handmade burgers, halal wings/options, monthly specialty burger, and pizza specials are concrete sales hooks.
3. **Press/social proof:** WGN, Chicago's Best, Windy City Live, Associated Press, Eat This Not That, Only In Illinois, Food on Demand, and podcast references create trust if presented cleanly.
4. **Operational truth:** OrderStart ordering, phone ordering, catering, dine-in/pickup/curbside/delivery, and separate full kitchen / cafe / gaming hours need to remain accurate.

## Conversion and credibility gaps

1. **Too many equal-priority CTAs.** The home page asks users to order, read the story, review the cook, view specials, see menu, sign up, read articles, and explore many sub-pages. A redesign should create a clear hierarchy: order now, see menu, catering/private chef, story/proof.
2. **Strong proof is scattered.** Press logos, clean-food promise, culinary training, quality guarantee, and specialty items exist, but are spread across dense sections instead of presented as one confident proof strip.
3. **Mobile density weakens action.** The mobile page is very long and text-heavy. Key CTAs and the address/phone are present, but the user has to scan through a lot of promo/story content to choose what to do.
4. **Menu content is deep but visually exhausting.** The menu page has strong items and specials but needs better grouping, spacing, scannability, and a more editorial treatment for signature items.
5. **Multiple contact emails create uncertainty.** The site shows both `Management@FratosKitchen.com` and `FratosPizza (at) gmail.com`; the build should avoid inventing one as the sole owner contact unless later confirmed.

## Mobile state

- Hero/first section has strong brand and order intent, but the page is visually busy and long on iPhone-sized viewports.
- CTA hierarchy needs simplification: phone/order/menu/catering should be immediately tappable and repeated as a sticky mobile action set.
- Menu and specials should become scannable cards/sections rather than dense text blocks.
- Hours/location/contact need a concise visit block so users do not have to search the long page for practical info.

## Build implications

- Recommended archetype direction remains **Cuisine**, personalized heavily toward a bold neighborhood culinary-kitchen/pizzeria identity rather than a generic family restaurant.
- Hero lock should combine: made-from-scratch culinary kitchen + Illinois-greatest mozzarella sticks / stone-oven pizza + Schaumburg + fast order CTA.
- The redesign should preserve the real OrderStart flow and phone number exactly.
- Do not over-polish away the eccentric Frato's voice; organize it into an owner-believable, sales-ready story.
