# Chicago Prime Steakhouse — sales battle cards

- Date: 2026-05-07
- Canonical gate: future `battle_cards`
- Status: **internal draft / not owner-facing yet**
- Archetype: **Heaven Palate**
- Build status: **not build-authorized yet** — pending founder approval, owner confirmations, MC lead/root workflow provisioning, protected agency API auth, and Heaven Palate source/template mapping.

## Core sales frame

Chicago Prime Steakhouse does not need a generic “new restaurant website.” It needs a premium, reservation-first digital experience that matches the restaurant’s actual value: prime steakhouse dining, private events, warm service, live lounge atmosphere, and a real family/hospitality legacy.

The strongest sales point is trust: the current website contains obvious template leftovers and CTA clutter, while the restaurant itself has credible premium assets. The redesign should make that gap impossible to miss.

## Battle card 1 — “Our current site already works.”

### Likely owner objection

“We already have a website with hours, reservations, menu, private events, and order links.”

### Response

“Yes — the useful pieces are there. The issue is that the site does not present them with the polish a premium steakhouse needs. Guests can see template leftovers, repeated CTAs, and placeholder content before they see a clean Reserve or Private Dining path. The redesign keeps the working links and turns them into a more trustworthy experience.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Current audit found bakery placeholders, `555-555-55-55`, slash placeholders, carousel placeholders, and duplicate copyright years.
- OpenTable/private dining/menu/order links exist but are cluttered.
- Mobile lacks a clear sticky Reserve / Call / Directions path.

### Demo move

Show the old homepage text/screenshot with placeholders, then show the new first screen where Reserve a Table and Private Dining are deliberate and premium.

## Battle card 2 — “We are family-owned, not stuffy.”

### Likely owner concern

“We do not want the site to feel cold, corporate, or pretentious.”

### Response

“That is why the direction is classic luxury with warmth, not corporate luxury. The site should look polished enough for private dining and date-night reservations, while keeping the family-owned Northwest Suburbs story, hospitality, and George Kalkounos legacy visible.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Official homepage says Chicago Prime is family-owned in the Northwest Suburbs and emphasizes personal touch.
- George A. Kalkounos page gives a real hospitality/family legacy.
- Reviews repeatedly mention friendly staff, attentive service, celebrations, and owner/staff hospitality.

### Demo move

Show the legacy/hospitality section immediately after the steakhouse/private dining proof, not as an isolated memorial page.

## Battle card 3 — “Will this help reservations?”

### Likely owner objection

“Does a redesign actually help people book?”

### Response

“The preview is built around reservation behavior. Reserve stays primary on desktop and mobile, while Menu, Directions, Call, and Private Dining stay close because those are the actions guests check before booking or choosing a steakhouse.”

### Proof to cite internally

- OpenTable path is already present and should be preserved.
- Audit found reservation is available but buried in repetitive CTA rows.
- Google reviews show special-occasion behavior: birthdays, celebrations, group dining, date-night style experiences.

### Demo move

Open mobile first. Show Reserve / Call / Directions sticky actions and the hero CTAs before scrolling.

## Battle card 4 — “Private dining matters.”

### Likely owner objection

“We do a lot of private events. Can the site support that?”

### Response

“Yes. Private Dining should be the second major conversion after Reserve. The current site has valuable room names and capacities, but the redesign should sell the occasion first — corporate dinners, milestones, rehearsal dinners, celebrations — then provide confirmed room details and a clear inquiry path.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Official private dining page lists Main Dining Room, Bar & Lounge, Walnut & State Room, Walnut Room, and State Room.
- `events@chicagoprimesteakhouse.com` and restaurant phone are present.
- Private dining policies are detailed but currently too operational for homepage-first selling.

### Demo move

Show a premium Private Dining section with room cards and inquiry CTA, while labeling capacity/policy details as owner-confirmation-needed before public launch.

## Battle card 5 — “Can we use reviews?”

### Likely owner objection

“Our reviews are strong. Can we show them?”

### Response

“We captured review evidence and can use the themes to guide the site: attentive service, celebrations, live music, steaks, seafood, wine, and hospitality. For public display, we should use exact sourced quotes or ratings only after approval so the site stays trustworthy.”

### Proof to cite internally

- 30 written Google reviews were captured from the Highest-rated Google Maps view.
- Visible Google summary at capture: `4.4 · 1,191 reviews`.
- Review packet includes real service/live music/celebration/food proof.

### Demo move

Show internal proof themes first. Only show direct quote placements if owner/founder approves exact sourced quote usage.

## Battle card 6 — “What about live music?”

### Likely owner objection

“The lounge and live entertainment are important.”

### Response

“Live music should be a support feature, not the whole brand. It gives dinner more atmosphere and helps the lounge/patio story, but the homepage should still lead with Reserve and Private Dining.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Live entertainment page says Wednesday–Saturday 7PM–11PM.
- Private dining text says Tuesday–Saturday, so schedule currentness must be confirmed.
- Reviews mention enjoying live entertainment.

### Demo move

Show a mid-page live lounge/patio section after the steakhouse/private dining sections, not a nightclub-style homepage.

## Battle card 7 — “Can we keep order online?”

### Likely owner objection

“We still want Uber Eats, Grubhub, and Toast available.”

### Response

“Absolutely. The redesign should keep those as utility links. They just should not compete visually with Reserve and Private Dining, because this is an upscale steakhouse and the higher-value guest journey is dine-in and events.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Current site includes Uber Eats, Grubhub, and Toast.
- Audit explicitly recommends lowering order-online hierarchy, not removing it.

### Demo move

Show provider links in footer/utility area and Reserve/Private Dining in the hero/action bar.

## Battle card 8 — “Can we use the legacy story?”

### Likely owner concern

“We want to honor George, but not make the site too heavy.”

### Response

“The legacy section should be respectful and concise. It can focus on hospitality, mentorship, family, and guests treated as friends, then link to the full story/scholarship page. The homepage should honor the story without turning into a memorial page.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Official George A. Kalkounos page includes immigrant hospitality story, restaurant career, scholarship, and guests-as-friends language.
- Builder brief bans exploitative grief language.

### Demo move

Show a short “A legacy of hospitality” module and a link to the full legacy page.

## Battle card 9 — “Do we need new photos?”

### Likely owner objection

“Can you just use what is online?”

### Response

“The strongest version uses owner-approved steak, dining room, lounge, patio, private room, exterior, wine/cocktail, and legacy imagery. If photo rights are not confirmed, we can design with replaceable image slots, but we should not imply final ownership of random online photos.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Builder brief requires owner-approved photography or clearly marked placeholders.
- Steakhouse/private dining sellability depends heavily on authentic room/food imagery.

### Demo move

Show image slots as placeholders labeled for owner-approved assets rather than presenting them as final.

## Battle card 10 — “Why Heaven Palate?”

### Likely owner/internal question

“Why this template direction instead of something trendier?”

### Response

“Chicago Prime is a classic steakhouse, so the site should feel mature, trusted, and reservation-led. Heaven Palate gives that dark luxury, serif typography, restrained CTA hierarchy, and premium dining-room rhythm. Trendier layouts would distract from the real value: steakhouse confidence, private dining, and hospitality.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Routing decision rejected Bamzi, Bramble, Cuisine, Roma, and Qitchen with specific fit reasons.
- Heaven Palate is the agency archetype for upscale steakhouse / formal dining / classic luxury.

### Demo move

Show hero + private dining + legacy sequence as a classic luxury conversion path.

## Battle card 11 — What should Ethan lead with?

### Recommended opener

“Your restaurant already feels like a premium local steakhouse. The website should stop looking like an unfinished template and start making reservations and private dining feel effortless.”

### Follow-up points

- “We kept the real OpenTable, phone, hours, address, and event inquiry paths.”
- “We made Reserve and Private Dining the visual hierarchy.”
- “We removed placeholder/template trust leaks.”
- “We gave the legacy story a respectful role instead of burying it.”
- “We treated live music and order-online links as useful support, not distractions.”
- “We did not invent awards, ratings, capacities, menu items, or image rights.”

## Red flags before owner-facing demo

Do not demo publicly if any of these are unresolved in the preview:

- Wrong OpenTable URL.
- Missing/broken phone, directions, menu, event email, provider, or social links.
- Any bakery/template placeholder content.
- Fake or unsourced rating/review count.
- “Award-winning” without approved source/wording.
- Private dining capacity claims without owner confirmation.
- Live music schedule shown before Tuesday-vs-Wednesday conflict is resolved.
- George legacy copy that feels exploitative or overly long.
- Stock images presented as restaurant-owned.
- Weak mobile Reserve / Call / Directions path.
- Delivery/order CTAs competing above the fold with Reserve and Private Dining.
