## Visual Thesis

Mago should read as a lively downtown Mexican cantina with real food craft, tequila depth, and group-night utility. The new first impression should sell what the current Arlington Heights page hides: tableside guacamole, margarita flights, moles, tacos, 200-plus tequilas, sidewalk seating, chef/family recipe credibility, and event-ready hospitality.

Use Bramble as warm editorial hospitality rather than a cuisine-color skin. The visual system should feel social, appetite-led, and polished enough for a downtown dinner, while still practical for reservations, order online, catering, and Fiesta Room group conversion.

## Hero Lock

- Hero image: use the clean production plate as the first preview hero background and future video-poster fallback.
- Composition: center-balanced cantina still life with molcajete guacamole as the anchor, margarita flight on the right, tacos/mole/tortillas in the lower field, and calm left-side negative space for coded copy.
- Crop guidance: `object-position: 55% center`; preserve the molcajete and margarita flight on mobile center-crop while keeping the left third available for overlay copy.
- Wordmark: `MAGO`
- Eyebrow: `ARLINGTON HEIGHTS CANTINA`
- Supporting line: `Tableside guacamole, margaritas, moles, and 200-plus tequilas.`
- Primary action: `Reserve`
- Secondary actions: `Order Online`, `Group Dining`
- Proof chip: `4.4 Google rating / 1,600-plus reviews` only after MC-verified count wording is accepted.
- Do not use generated text baked into the production hero. All final hero copy, CTAs, proof, and navigation should be coded HTML.

## Palette

- Near black mole: `#120B07`
- Deep wood: `#2A140B`
- Warm tortilla cream: `#F1D9A5`
- Marigold: `#E5A21A`
- Paprika red: `#B54027`
- Agave green: `#6F8B35`
- Guacamole green: `#91A52F`
- Salt white: `#F7EEE0`

Use tortilla cream and salt white for legible type on darker hero fields, marigold for active stamps, paprika red for restrained conversion accents, and agave/guacamole greens for food and tequila cues. Avoid candy-bright taqueria saturation, dark luxury speakeasy styling, generic black-and-gold, purple/blue gradients, and one-note beige.

## Typography

- Display: refined Mexican editorial serif or sturdy decorative display with broad, warm forms. The wordmark should feel restaurant-owned and festive, not nightclub, cartoon, or fast-casual.
- Labels: tracked sans or small caps for eyebrow text, proof chips, hours, and section labels.
- Body: readable modern sans with compact line lengths and strong contrast.

Use the large restaurant name as the anchor. Keep operational text tighter and calmer so the page can carry several conversion paths without feeling noisy.

## Section Language

Lead with concrete, verified reasons to visit: tableside guacamole, margarita flight, tequila depth, moles, tacos, skirt steak/seafood where menu evidence supports it, downtown Arlington Heights location, sidewalk seating, two dining rooms, and the Fiesta Room for 20-100 guests.

Use review language around friendly service, special occasions, repeat visits, food quality, drinks, and downtown room energy. Avoid invented founder history, generic "authentic Mexican" filler, or unsourced awards and superlatives.

## Build Notes

- Preserve the multi-anchor conversion floor: desktop floating CTA pill plus mobile bottom action bar.
- Mobile sticky actions should prioritize call, directions, reserve, order online, and group dining/events.
- Split menu preview into Food and Drinks/Tequila, with feature chips for tableside guacamole, moles, tacos, margarita flight, tequila selection, sangria, and specials.
- Add an opening-times/location band for Arlington Heights with address, phone, hours, directions, reserve, and order links.
- Include a Fiesta Room/private-events block with verified capacity language: 20-100 guests, family-style menus, bar packages, and event planning assistance.
- Surface chef/family-recipe credibility through Juan Luis Gonzalez, Ric Munoz, Grandma Paula recipes, moles, and the 200-plus tequila program only where public evidence supports the wording.
- Use scrapbook/photo-strip energy sparingly: review cards, menu stamps, warm paper texture, tile details, and agave/paprika accents.
- Keep cards for repeated menu, proof, and event items only; do not put page sections inside floating card containers.

## Motion Notes

Use warm cantina aliveness: candle and glass highlight shimmer, margarita condensation glints, subtle photo-strip drift, gentle section reveals, and restrained hover lift on menu/proof cards. For later image-to-video, use the clean plate as the exact first frame and animate only small natural motion: glass highlights, citrus glints, faint steam/heat near food, and candlelike light movement. Camera should stay locked.

## Guardrails

- No humans, faces, arms, or hands in generated hero assets.
- No readable logos, labels, signage, UI, or generated typography in the clean production plate.
- No fast-casual taco shop treatment, dark ceremonial fine dining, or generic Cinco de Mayo decoration.
- No fake review quotes, invented owner claims, or unverified counts in final coded copy.
- The website should make reservations, online ordering, group dining, catering, directions, and call actions easy to find from the first viewport.

The design reference prompt asked for a warm Bramble-style editorial cantina still life with molcajete guacamole, margarita flight, tacos, tortillas, mole, lime, agave green glass, and minimal `MAGO` reference typography.
