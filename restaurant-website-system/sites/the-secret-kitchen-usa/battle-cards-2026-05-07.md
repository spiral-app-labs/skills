# The Secret Kitchen USA — sales battle cards

- Date: 2026-05-07
- Canonical gate: future `battle_cards`
- Status: **internal draft / not owner-facing yet**
- Archetype: **Bamzi**
- Build status: **not build-authorized yet** — pending founder approval, owner confirmations, MC lead/root workflow provisioning, and protected agency API auth.

## Core sales frame

The Secret Kitchen USA does not need a generic restaurant website. It needs a premium reservation-first digital experience that makes Chef Aanal Kotak’s U.S. debut, modern Indian menu, and dining-room drama obvious in the first few seconds.

## Battle card 1 — “Our current site already looks nice.”

### Likely owner objection

“Our site already has nice visuals and information. Why redesign it?”

### Response

“Yes — the current site has good raw material. The issue is that it does not convert that material into a clear dinner decision fast enough. A guest should immediately know why this is special, how to reserve, what to order, and how to get there. Right now, those pieces are present but scattered.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Current audit found the hero is atmospheric but passive.
- Reserve/Menu/Directions are not dominant enough above the fold.
- Chef story is commercially strong but separated from the homepage flow.
- Menu depth is real but under-presented.

### Demo move

Show the new hero with Reserve Now / Explore Menu / Directions visible immediately. Then scroll to the Chef Aanal story and menu journey.

## Battle card 2 — “We are not just another Indian restaurant.”

### Owner concern

“We do not want to be positioned like a generic Indian restaurant.”

### Response

“That is exactly why the redesign uses a cinematic Bamzi structure instead of a generic local-restaurant layout. The site centers Chef Aanal, handmade spices, global flavors, and the U.S.-debut story, then uses the menu as a curated journey rather than a list of curry categories.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Official site positions the restaurant around Indian heritage + global flavors.
- Chef page says Chef Aanal personally creates spices for The Secret Kitchen.
- Official presence: India, Australia, Canada, USA.
- Reviews emphasize modern/upscale dining, presentation, decor, and memorable experience.

### Demo move

Show chef/story section and menu chapter headings. Avoid saying “authentic curry near me.”

## Battle card 3 — “Will this help reservations?”

### Likely owner objection

“Does this actually help guests book?”

### Response

“The preview is built around reservation behavior. On mobile and desktop, Reserve is never buried. Menu and Directions stay close because those are the two things guests check before booking or leaving.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Current reservation path exists but is not emphasized strongly enough.
- Reviews include reservation/timing feedback, which means clarity matters.
- Official GetSeat path is available and should be preserved exactly unless owner confirms a change.

### Demo move

Open mobile view first. Show sticky Reserve / Menu / Directions flow.

## Battle card 4 — “Can we show the full menu?”

### Likely owner objection

“Our menu is large. We do not want it oversimplified.”

### Response

“The homepage should not replace the full menu. It should make the menu feel exciting and easy to enter. The redesign highlights signature chapters and keeps a full-menu path for guests who want the complete list.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Official menu includes Hot Bar, Indian Street Food, First Plate, Indo-Chinese, World Cuisine, Main Plate Veg/Non-Veg, Sweet Plate, Traditional Platter, Junior Plate, Comfort Plate, Bread Bar.
- Review/menu proof points include avocado dahi puri, lamb chops, butter chicken, tandoori prawns, gulab jamun, mocktails, vegetarian selection.

### Demo move

Show menu journey: Street Food Reimagined, Tandoor & First Plates, Royal Mains, Sweet Theatre, then full-menu CTA.

## Battle card 5 — “What about private dining or banquets?”

### Likely owner objection

“We do events/private dining too.”

### Response

“We can absolutely include that, but only with accurate details. Before publishing it, we should confirm capacities, minimums, policies, and the right inquiry path so the site does not create operational confusion.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Current source packet flags private dining/banquet language as tentative.
- Builder brief requires careful celebration/group language until owner details are confirmed.

### Demo move

Show a cautious “Celebrations & group dinners” section and mark private dining copy as owner-confirmation-needed.

## Battle card 6 — “Can we use reviews?”

### Likely owner objection

“Can we show our Google reviews?”

### Response

“We captured review evidence and can use the themes to guide the copy. For public display, we should decide exactly how you want ratings, review counts, and quotes used so everything is sourced and approved.”

### Proof to cite internally

- 30 written Google reviews were captured from the Highest-rated Google Maps view.
- Visible rating summary at capture: 4.7 from 161 reviews.
- Risk note: do not publish ratings/review counts/direct quotes without approved sourced placement.

### Demo move

Show internal review themes, not direct quote placement, unless approved.

## Battle card 7 — “Do we need new photos?”

### Likely owner objection

“Can you just use photos from the internet or our current site?”

### Response

“The best version uses owner-approved food, dining room, bar, dessert, chef, and exterior imagery. If rights are not confirmed, the design can use replaceable image slots, but we should not imply we own or have permission for photos that are not approved.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Builder brief requires photo-rights-safe layouts.
- Audit flags photo/press rights as a pre-build confirmation.

### Demo move

Show image slots as “owner-approved photo goes here” rather than pretending placeholder imagery is final.

## Battle card 8 — “Why Bamzi instead of a classic luxury design?”

### Likely owner objection

“Should the site look more classic luxury?”

### Response

“A classic luxury template would make the restaurant feel like a steakhouse or hotel dining room. Bamzi keeps the premium feeling but adds the cinematic pacing this concept needs: chef story, global flavors, jewel-toned atmosphere, dramatic menu, and a stronger reserve path.”

### Proof to cite internally

- Routing decision rejected Heaven Palate because it would flatten the modern Indian/global-spice identity.
- Bamzi supports dark-light-dark narrative rhythm and poster-like hero treatment.

### Demo move

Show hero + chef + menu sequence as one cinematic story.

## Battle card 9 — “What should Ethan lead with?”

### Recommended opener

“You already have a restaurant that feels like a destination. The redesign is about making the website feel that way too — and making it easier for guests to reserve, explore the menu, and plan dinner from their phone.”

### Follow-up points

- “We kept the real business facts intact.”
- “We elevated Chef Aanal’s story instead of burying it.”
- “We turned the menu into a guided dinner journey.”
- “We made Reserve/Menu/Directions the conversion spine.”
- “We avoided fake ratings, unapproved awards, and unconfirmed private dining claims.”

## Battle card 10 — Red flags before owner-facing demo

Do not demo publicly if any of these are unresolved in the preview:

- Wrong reservation URL.
- Missing or broken phone/email/directions/social links.
- Fake or unsourced Google rating/review count.
- “Award-winning” without approved wording.
- Private dining/capacity claims without owner confirmation.
- Generic Indian restaurant copy.
- Stock images presented as restaurant-owned.
- Weak mobile Reserve/Menu/Directions path.
- Menu depth reduced to a few generic dishes.

## Close-the-loop checklist for sales handoff

Before Ethan uses these battle cards externally:

1. Confirm the MC lead/root workflow exists.
2. Attach audit, reviews, routing, builder brief, pitch, and battle-card artifacts to MC.
3. Confirm owner-approved photo rights.
4. Confirm menu/prices/currentness.
5. Confirm private dining details or remove private dining claims.
6. Confirm awards/press permissions or omit them.
7. Run final desktop/mobile QA on the preview.
8. Verify all links: reservation, menu, phone, email, directions, Instagram, Facebook.
