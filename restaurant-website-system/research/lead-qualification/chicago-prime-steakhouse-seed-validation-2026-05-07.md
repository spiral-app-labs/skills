## Website friction / sellability opportunity

Current official-site issues visible from text fetches:

- Homepage has a premium business but thin narrative; it does not immediately organize the steakhouse experience into a strong reserve/private dining story.
- Site content exposes obvious template residue: “Downtown bakery,” “Uptown bakery,” placeholder phone `555-555-55-55`, and repeated slash placeholders appear in fetched navigation/page text.
- Footer copyright text appears duplicated (`2024` and `2026`).
- Review carousel includes “Slide title / Write your caption here” placeholders.
- Reservation, call, and order CTAs exist, but the hierarchy appears repetitive rather than polished.
- Private dining has strong revenue-driving details, but the homepage does not appear to elevate it as a premium event funnel.
- Founder/legacy story is strong but separated from the main conversion path.
- Menu is PDF-forward; a modern preview could turn the menu into an elegant steakhouse/wine/private-event journey while preserving full menu links.

## Recommended archetype: Heaven Palate

Use **Heaven Palate** because this is an upscale steakhouse with classic fine-dining positioning, reservations, wine/private events, and a premium hospitality legacy.

Why not other archetypes:

- **Bamzi**: Live music/lounge energy exists, but the core business is a classic steakhouse and private dining destination, not a trendy cinematic concept needing high-contrast narrative drama.
- **Bramble**: The lounge/live music piece is secondary; positioning the whole restaurant like a bar would underplay fine dining and private events.
- **Cuisine**: Too casual/family-forward for a steakhouse with private dining, valet/policies, wine, and upscale reservation behavior.
- **Roma**: There is a founder story, but not enough seasonal/chef-editorial restraint to route away from classic steakhouse luxury.
- **Qitchen**: Not an omakase/high-design concept.

## Preview thesis

Create a classic, polished, reservation-first steakhouse preview that:

1. Makes “Reserve a Table” unmistakable above the fold.
2. Elevates private dining as a high-value conversion path.
3. Brings the George Kalkounos hospitality legacy into the homepage story.
4. Clarifies live lounge entertainment without turning the site into a nightlife brand.
5. Preserves OpenTable, call, directions, menu PDFs, private-dining inquiry, and order-online provider handoffs.
