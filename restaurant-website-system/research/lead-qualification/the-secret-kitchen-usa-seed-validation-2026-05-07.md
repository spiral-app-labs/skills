## Why this is sellable

The Secret Kitchen is unusually pitchable because the public story is already strong: an international modern Indian fine-dining concept by Chef Aanal Kotak, with presence across India, Australia, Canada, and now the United States. The Schaumburg location is described in press as the concept's first U.S. restaurant and has a high-design, photo-forward dining room: jewel-green marble, carved botanical woodwork, crimson velvet, brass/emerald details, flamingo mosaics, and tableside dessert drama.

That creates a website-agency opportunity different from the usual “bad local site” lead. The pitch is not that they lack a website; it is that a premium U.S. debut deserves a web experience that feels as cinematic, modern, and reservation-worthy as the restaurant itself. The current official site contains the right facts and conversion paths, but the fetched pages are structurally thin: the homepage reduces the brand to one paragraph, reservation/contact pages repeat navigation/footer content heavily, and the menu fetch surfaced only dessert items rather than a clear full-menu story. A Bamzi-based preview could make the royal Indian / global fine-dining story feel intentional and sellable without inventing claims.

## Archetype route

Use exactly one base archetype: **Bamzi**.

Why:

- The brand is maximalist, cinematic, and high-drama: royal Indian inspiration, jewel tones, brass/velvet/onyx, photo-forward interiors, tableside flambé, and international-chef credibility.
- The restaurant needs dark/light narrative pacing and visual theater more than a restrained editorial layout.
- A strong Bamzi preview can carry the U.S. debut story, chef authority, dining-room drama, and reservation CTA in a way that feels premium but not generic fine dining.

Why not Roma:

- Roma is too restrained and seasonal/editorial for this concept. The Secret Kitchen is chef-led, but the public identity is theatrical, royal, and sensory rather than quiet, sparse, or farm-seasonal.

Why not Heaven Palate:

- Heaven Palate fits classic luxury and reservation-led steakhouse/formal dining. The Secret Kitchen needs more color, rhythm, motion, and cultural specificity than that template's classic-luxury register.

Avoid:

- Generic “Indian restaurant near me” language.
- Flattening the restaurant into standard curry/biryani tropes.
- Inventing awards, reviews, or Chicago expansion claims beyond sourced press.
- Overusing “royal” in a costume-like way; keep the tone modern, elegant, and grounded in the actual chef/story/interior evidence.
- Publishing menu prices or full item lists without fresh official verification.

## Proposed preview direction

1. Hero: cinematic dining-room / jewel-tone visual treatment, “Modern Indian fine dining arrives in Schaumburg,” direct Reserve CTA.
2. Chef/story: Chef Aanal Kotak, international presence, handmade spices, royal-kitchen inspiration.
3. Menu journey: narrative chapters rather than a flat PDF/menu dump — First Plate, Indo-Chinese, World Cuisine, Main Plates, Sweet Plate.
4. Signature moments: Avocado Dahi Puri, Thecha Paneer Tikka, #TSKfied Butter Chicken, Nalli Gosht, Gulab Jamun Flambé, Kesar Mango Rasmalai.
5. Visit/reserve: hours, address, phone, GetSeat reservation path, map/directions.
6. Social/photo cues: throne/photo moment, flamingo mosaic bar, emerald/brass/crimson palette, tableside dessert.
